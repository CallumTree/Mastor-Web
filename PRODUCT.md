# Product

## Platform

web

Installable PWA (portrait, standalone). Mobile web on site and desktop browser in the office are both first-class. It is not a native app.

## Users

Mastor is for **UK contractors and their teams on schedule-of-rates work who don't have a back office behind them**: planned programmes, repairs and maintenance, and empty properties for councils, housing associations, NHS trusts, police and fire services, schools, colleges and private clients. Anyone from one person doing the lot to a team across several jobs, usually running the money side in Excel today (master schedule, subbie comparison, valuation tabs). The front page talks to the business and everyone in it, not to one job title, and names no client's own frameworks or programmes. **Mastor is its own business: it names no contractor, client or council anywhere (the app, the front page, what new members see, the connector, the docs and the tests), and claims no link to any firm, unless the owner says otherwise.** Examples use made-up names (Example Builders, Example Council, Anytown). The owner's own account (their company name, jobs and documents) is their data as a customer and is never changed for this.

People who use it every day:

- **Director / QS (office):** prices work from the SoR, builds valuations and monthly applications, raises VAT invoices, keeps the variation register, watches margin and chases payment. Laptop or desktop, long careful sessions where the figures must hold up.
- **Site manager (on site):** site diary, photos, variations and dayworks as they are found, completes small works orders. Phone outdoors, often in glare and gloves, sometimes no signal.
- **Operatives:** log work, hours and photos. Brief visits, no training. **Never see rates, values or margins.**

People who only see the outputs:

- **The client** (a council or housing association): never opens the app but receives its PDFs — Variation Register, valuation certificates, VAT invoices, dayworks sheets and completion evidence packs. These have to pass a client QS's scrutiny.

Membership roles: owner, director, qs, site_manager, operative. Owners and directors manage the team; operatives see no money.

## Product Purpose

Mastor runs the commercial side of SoR framework work from start to finish: works order / BoQ priced against the framework SoR → site diary, dayworks and variations → completion evidence → interim valuation or monthly application → VAT invoice → payment, with cost and margin alongside. Each figure traces back to its source. Success means a contractor claims everything they're owed, knows what each job makes, can defend every line to the client, and isn't let down by the app on site.

## Positioning

- **The master schedule, in one place — hence MΛSTΘR.** Built from a day-to-day sticking point: a job's money and record spread across endless spreadsheets, OneNote, planners and sticky notes. Mastor puts the job — scope, variations, diary, notes, documents, valuations, invoices — in one spot. For small and family-run firms that don't have a QS or haven't scaled yet: it does the intermediary work a QS would, simply and cheaply, and grows with the firm (team, roles, more jobs) without changing how it works.
- **Replaces the SoR contractor's spreadsheet stack.** Trade job apps are built for domestic quotes and know nothing about SoR codes, uplifts, client VOs or valuations; enterprise construction software is priced and staffed for firms with a commercial department. Mastor sits in that gap.
- **Never invents numbers.** Unmeasured quantities show as "not measured", lines without a rate as "unpriced" (never £0), a missing PO stays blank and flagged. Rates come from the contractor's own imported schedule, exactly as printed.
- **Site capture runs the valuation.** The original idea: capture on site as it happens (photos, video, voice) and Mastor picks out what's done, what's extra and what happened — read against the scope, approved by a person, straight into the valuation.
- **Finds money.** Checks the client's BoQ rates against the framework SoR, and shows margin per job against subbie and labour costs.
- **One way to do each thing.** Simple on purpose; capability is added without alternative paths.

Not now (parked deliberately): formal CVR (accruals, cost codes, period locks, forecast final accounts) — the margin view stays a simple "am I making money on this job", not a finance system. JCT/NRM work led by private QSs and architects — retention, QS-issued interim certificates, payment and pay-less notices. The bridge, when the SoR corner is won, is importing a priced bill with lump sums and provisional sums. RAMS and programmes stay in the contractor's existing documents; Mastor only stores them against the job.

## Operating Context

- Work arrives as framework works orders: a BoQ in Excel or PDF with SoR codes, quantities and rates and two uplift percentages (planned work), or a stream of small orders for one property each (responsive / voids).
- A planned job's pack from the client: a spec list (all properties and workstreams), one works order per contract, the PCI and any surveys. **The works order is the source of truth** — it comes direct from the client and carries the contract value; the spec list is the cross-check.
- Every client lays its documents out its own way: councils, housing associations, NHS trusts, police and fire services, schools, main contractors. The scope reader (`src/lib/scopeTable.ts`) works out a schedule of works, BoQ, scope of works or works order it has never seen, from what its columns mean and what's in them, whatever they're called ("Item", "Asset Ref", "Description of works", "UoM", "Unit price", "Total £"). It reads Excel (every sheet; a sheet per house or trade names its lines), CSV, Word tables and PDF tables, priced or not. Headings give the property, room or trade, wrapped descriptions are joined, and totals, collections, VAT and uplifts are left out. Figures are copied as printed and never worked out: a missing rate or quantity stays blank and is flagged. The client's own exact layouts (`sheet.ts`, `pdfTable.ts`, `worksOrder.ts`) are tried first. Only a document it can't read exactly goes to the AI reader (`api/parse-boq.js`), whose lines are flagged for checking. When a whole pack is sorted, only tables whose lines carry quantities or money count as a schedule, so a survey or register set out in a table stays with the documents.
- The framework SoR (e.g. M3NHF v8 Planned and Responsive, plus client additional rates) is imported once into the company's rate books.
- A new job starts from the client's pack (`src/screens/NewJob.tsx`, `src/lib/packJob.ts`): choose the files and the job's name, address, properties, contract ref and works-order contract value come from what they print, and the client's name from the PCI when its quote checks out. The person types only what the pack doesn't say (usually the PO number and the uplifts), then one tap creates the job with its lines, documents, notes and VOs. A responsive contract, or a job with no pack yet, is set up by hand.
- Variations are found on site, priced from the SoR, then sent for instruction. The client issues its own VO reference (scan, photo, handwritten ticket). Nobody sets a VO's status: it follows the facts (`voStage` in `src/lib/chase.tsx`), going logged → priced → with the client → instructed (a client ref, or a signed dayworks sheet) → claimed. The one thing a person says is "Client rejected it". Dayworks are priced from the SoR labour rates.
- **A VO is work at a house, so it lives in Scope with that house's lines** (`src/screens/Scope.tsx`): one list of everything to do at No. 3, its variations first and always open, so a gutter VO isn't missed before the scaffold comes down. Each VO carries its house (`Variation.property`; older ones are read from their "where", `voHouse`), is ticked into the valuation like a line once priced, and counts in the house's total. Scope adds and imports both: "Import scope", "Import VOs" (a client's VO schedule laid out like a spec list, read by the same reader: a VO per line at its house, priced as printed, instructed when the client's ref is given, the schedule kept as each VO's evidence), "Add line", "Add VO" (the house picked by one tap). The VOs filter shows the variation register at a glance; the register PDF, a client's VO instruction and dayworks are in Scope's menu.
- Notes are written on the day, in the diary. The day's note box takes what happened; "File as" Client, Commercial or H&S turns it into a note of its own on that day (H&S notes pin themselves). The job's home shows only pinned notes, and "All notes" finds any note on the job, the days' own notes included.
- Site captures are read on their own (`src/lib/autoRead.ts`). While the office has Mastor open, a job-day's photos, video, voice notes and day note are read against the scope once they've uploaded and nothing new has come in for 45 minutes, or once the day is over. It only reads the last week, one day at a time, within the daily AI limit, and it stops until the next open when it can't go on. The checks are strict because nobody looks before a suggestion is made (`verifySuggestions`). "Done" needs words: a photo alone can't show that a line is finished or which house it's in. On a scheme of several houses, "done" must name the house and only counts that house's lines. A quote that wasn't said drops the suggestion. Every suggestion still waits in Vals for a person.
- Valuations are numbered per job; one Open at a time; Issued ones are locked; each can have one VAT invoice. A VAT invoice names who it's to: it can't be created until the job has the client's name and invoice address (`jobMissingForInvoice`), printed under the client's name and carried into the Xero export. Terms run from the invoice date; part payments are tracked. Invoices export to Xero or QuickBooks.
- Site use: phone outdoors, intermittent signal, short sessions. Office use: desktop, long sessions, close checking.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- React 18 + Vite + TypeScript. Supabase for auth, sync and team functions, Row Level Security per company. IndexedDB on device; works offline once signed in and syncs later. Vercel deploys from `main`; previews per branch. Serverless AI readers in `api/parse-boq.js`, `api/parse-vo.js` and `api/read-pack.js` (PCI and surveys: every item quotes its document and the quote is checked on the device) and `api/read-site.js` (site captures: Gemini transcribes voice and video, Claude reads them against the scope; suggestions only, approved in Vals), gated to signed-in members with a daily limit per company. Mastor is also a Claude connector (`/api/mcp`, built from `server/mcp/` by `npm run build:mcp`): Claude signs in as the person through Supabase Auth's OAuth server (consent at `/oauth/consent`) and reads and writes with their own rights, so Row Level Security and the operative rule apply. Its figures and rules come from the same `src/lib` code as the screens. Its changes (`server/mcp/writes.ts`) are saved as the phones' sync saves them and marked `byClaude`; the app shows “by Claude” until a person saves the record. Issuing valuations and creating invoices are never done through it. The same tools run inside the app as Clerk, the assistant tab (`/api/agent`, built from `server/agent/` by the same command): Claude reads with the person's own sign-in and stops at every change, which the phone shows as a card that runs only when the person taps Do it. Each question counts once against the company's daily AI limit. To keep Mastor cheap for small firms, everyday questions go to the low-cost model (Claude Haiku 5.5, `ANTHROPIC_AGENT_MODEL`): Mastor works out every figure and Clerk only quotes them. Once a conversation opens a document, the rest of it goes to Claude Opus 5.5 (`ANTHROPIC_AGENT_DOC_MODEL`), and anything Haiku declines is tried once on Opus. The tool list is the same for every firm and is cached once for all of them, and each question logs its calls and tokens by model in the function log. To price a VO, Clerk looks the work up with `find_rate` (`server/mcp/rates.ts`): the job's own schedule of rates (rate books synced from the app) and the same work already priced on the company's jobs, and it shows where the rate came from (kept as the VO's `rateBook`). It never makes up a rate: no match means the VO is logged unpriced. The job's documents (Documents on the job home: the client's purchase order and pack, the programme, RAMS, certificates, drawings) are its source of truth: Clerk reads them with `read_document` (`server/mcp/docs.ts`) and quotes them by name and page. A PDF with a text layer is read as text, line by line; a scan goes to Claude as the PDF itself; Word, Excel and photos are read too. Purchase orders carry money, so operatives neither see nor read them. If the app's figures differ from a document (the PO number or value, the uplifts), Clerk says so and changes nothing unless asked. Filing a purchase order offers "Check with Clerk": Clerk reads the order, compares the PO number, the PO value (the order's total net, excluding VAT) and the uplifts in the order they compound with the job, and offers `update_job` with the figures as printed; afterwards it runs the app's own BoQ-to-PO check. Job changes by Claude are marked `byClaude` until a person saves the job. The Diary is the job's line (`src/screens/Plan.tsx`, `src/lib/plan.ts`): site days behind, today, and the plan ahead. The plan is to-dos (dated or not, ticked off by anyone) and key dates (a start, a delivery, a test), each optionally for a property and trade, and a to-do can lead up to a key date so each shows the other. They are diary records (types `todo` and `event`), so they sync with no new table. Office-only items never reach operatives. The jobs screen shows the week across every job, with office roles also seeing Mastor's own deadlines (overdue valuations, VOs late with the client). Clerk reads the plan in `get_job` and books dates and to-dos (`add_to_plan`) or ticks them off (`tick_off`), each through a Do it card. Valuations keep a monthly rhythm (`src/lib/rhythm.ts`): they go in during the first week of each month, and a month with less than two weeks' work since the last one was sent (or since the job started) waits for the next, so the open valuation only becomes the next step in its week; "Wait till next month" moves it on (`job.valWait`). A sent valuation then goes through the client's review (`valuation.review`): with the client, changes asked (it reopens to amend, and ticks go into it until it's sent again), agreed, then invoiced. Payment terms run from the invoice; valuations sent before the review step keep running from their issue date. The review is recorded in the app by a person; Clerk reads the valuation week and each valuation's stage in `get_valuations`.
- Front page at `/` (index.html → `src/landing/`), for firms that haven't joined. It leads with fifty numbered founding places (free through the pilot, then half price for as long as they stay; no price shown until one is set). Sign-ups go through `api/early-signup.js` to the `claim_founding_place` function (supabase/schema.sql v7), which hands out places one at a time and is counted live; the owner is emailed via Resend when `RESEND_API_KEY` and `SIGNUP_NOTIFY_TO` are set. The app lives at `/app` (app.html → `src/main.tsx`); the installed app, anyone signed in on the browser and sign-in links from emails skip the front page (`/?site` always shows it).
- Screens: Jobs board, Dashboard, New job (from the client's pack), Job view (Home, Diary, Scope, Vals in the dock; there is no separate VOs page, the capture key raised over it, Clerk in the job's header; Costs), Notes, Settings, Team, Schedule of rates, BoQ import, VO import, Dayworks, sign-in gate.
- Business rules: VO numbers per job (highest + 1), never reused. Invoice numbers never reused; the sequence can continue the company's existing one. Valuations and VOs in Roman numerals (VAL III, VO XII). Rate books are imported whole and never edited.
- Terminology: SoR / schedule of rates, rate book, works order, BoQ, VO, dayworks, PO, uplift, valuation (VAL), application, PPR, WHQS, responsive, void, workstream, property, margin.
- Undecided: pricing (pilots first), card billing.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Mastor**. Wordmark **MΛSTΘR**: Cinzel Roman capitals, a hand-drawn Λ and a Θ whose bar reads as a spirit level (`src/components/Wordmark.tsx`). Everything else uses Archivo (normal width for reading and headings, semi-condensed uppercase for markings) and JetBrains Mono for every figure, code, ref and quantity.
- App icon: a Roman masonry arch, copper on charcoal (`public/icon.svg`).
- Classical thread: Roman numerals for valuations and VOs; opening titles with the arch and keystone, played once on first open.
- Visual language: the drawing office (see DESIGN.md), chosen by the user in Nov 2026 from three dark directions. An ink-navy drafting sheet with a faint grid, milled-steel panels and hairlines, every data block drawn twice like a title block; white type for the facts; two signals only: cyan for the selection (the next step, variations, by Claude), gold for what needs you (overdue, to-do), told apart by the sign's shape. The front page carries the same look; the client's PDFs stay print-white.
- Voice: plain, direct builder's English (en-GB). Never overstates.

## Evidence on Hand

- Test fixtures (`tests/boq-trades.tsv`, `tests/boq-rooms.tsv` and the rest) carry made-up names, addresses and references. No client's documents and no licensed schedule of rates are stored in the repository, and no test reads them.
- **Not on hand:** customer testimonials, outside users, usage metrics, pricing or press. These must not be fabricated.

## Product Principles

1. **Truth over tidiness.** Show unknowns as unknown. An honest blank beats a plausible number.
2. **Every figure traces to its source.** A client QS can follow any line back to the SoR, the BoQ, the VO or the instruction.
3. **Find the money.** Underpaid rates, unclaimed variations, unpriced dayworks and thin margins are surfaced, not buried.
4. **One way to do each thing.** Add capability without adding alternative paths.
5. **Site-proof.** Works on a phone, outdoors, with no signal, for someone who hasn't been trained.
6. **The outputs are the product.** The PDFs the client receives deserve as much care as the screens.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Outdoor phone use calls for legible contrast in bright light, large touch targets that work with gloves, and respect for reduced motion. WCAG 2.2 AA is the working assumption.
